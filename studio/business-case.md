## 1. Executive summary

The single most reliable pattern in technology entrepreneurship of the last 20 years
is **geographic arbitrage of proven business models**: take a model that has already
survived product-market-fit risk in the US and rebuild it for a market it hasn't
reached, adapted to local regulation, language and distribution. Rocket Internet
industrialized this and — despite its failures — produced Zalando (IPO at $5.9B),
HelloFresh (IPO at €1.7B) and Delivery Hero (peak ~$22B) from a single Berlin factory.

The venture-studio structure has since matured into the best-performing early-stage
asset class on record: studio-built startups reach seed in ~11 months (vs ~36),
reach Series A at a ~72–84% rate (vs ~42%), and show portfolio IRRs around 53%
(vs ~21% for conventional early-stage portfolios).

**Schmelz combines the two**: the replica thesis (kill idea risk before writing code)
executed through the studio machine (kill execution risk with shared infrastructure),
domiciled in the EU jurisdiction with the strongest structural advantages for exactly
this vehicle — Luxembourg.

Six-year targets: 9 ventures launched, 5 alive at Series A quality, portfolio
holding NAV €55–80M on €8M invested, studio opco self-funding from year 4 via
management fees and secondaries.

## 2. Why replicas — the strategic rationale

### 2.1 The risk math

A de-novo startup carries three stacked risks: **(a)** does anyone want this,
**(b)** do the unit economics work, **(c)** can this team execute. A replica of a
US company that has already reached $100M+ ARR deletes (a) and most of (b). What
remains — localization and execution — is precisely what a studio's shared machine
(engineering platform, legal, GTM playbooks, hiring bench) is built to do repeatably.

### 2.2 The Rocket Internet evidence — both directions

What worked: speed as the weapon (launch in ~100 days), centralized platform teams,
serial-EIR founders, television-era distribution innovations the originals hadn't
tried. What failed: chasing emerging markets with weak unit economics (Jumia),
growth-at-all-costs (€1B aggregate portfolio EBITDA loss in 2015), and reputational
drag from hostile "clone and flip to the original" tactics.

**Schmelz's corrections, learned from the record:**
1. **Rich-market discipline** — EU/EEA only; no emerging-market adventures.
2. **Unit-economics gate** — a replica must show contribution-margin-positive paths
   by month 18 at Luxembourg cost structures, or it dies in diligence.
3. **Regulated-moat preference** — we deliberately choose models where EU regulation
   (CSSF, DORA, AMLR, EU AI Act, e-invoicing mandates) *raises* the localization
   wall behind us. Rocket cloned e-commerce, where the moat was only capital;
   we clone compliance-heavy B2B, where the moat is the rulebook.
4. **Build-to-own, not build-to-flip** — no adversarial exits to the original;
   originals become partners or acquirers on friendly terms (several US firms want
   EU distribution but won't build EU-sovereign infrastructure — we are their
   entry, or their competitor, at our option).

### 2.3 The timing argument (why 2026 is the best replica window since 2010)

- **AI-service arbitrage**: US-proven "AI-native services" models (Harvey $190M ARR
  in legal, Basis $1.15B in accounting, PipeDreams-style AI rollups of trades) are
  1–3 years old and have **no European equivalents at scale**. The window before EU
  natives emerge is open now and closes fast.
- **EU regulatory divergence**: DORA, AMLR, the EU AI Act, and mandatory B2B
  e-invoicing (rolling out across member states) mean US originals **cannot ship
  their product into the EU unchanged** — the replica isn't a copy, it's the
  compliant fork, and the original is structurally late.
- **Succession wave**: Europe mirrors the US's 2.3M boomer-owned businesses seeking
  exits — fragmented fiduciaires, trades, and service firms with no buyers. The US
  "buy-then-automate" rollup playbook transfers with *less* competition for deals.

## 3. Why Luxembourg — the unfair advantages

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **SOPARFI holding regime** | The studio's holding entity as a Luxembourg SOPARFI: participation exemption on qualifying dividends and capital gains — portfolio exits compound largely tax-free inside the vehicle. This is why 40%+ of EU PE/VC structures already sit in Luxembourg; we simply use the home advantage natively |
| **Grant stacking per venture** | Each venture is a separate Luxembourg company: Fit4Start (€150k) + young-innovative-enterprise and RDI aid are per-company. Nine ventures ≈ €1.5–2.5M of non-dilutive capital across the portfolio |
| **Ecosystem density** | LHoFT, Luxinnovation, Digital Tech Fund, LBAN, Luxembourg Future Fund (EIF-backed) — all within walking distance; the studio becomes their recurring deal-flow supplier |
| **Regulated-industry credibility** | For compliance-heavy replicas (our preference), "domiciled where the regulator lives" is a sales asset no Berlin or Paris studio has for CSSF-perimeter products |
| **Multilingual launch pad** | FR/DE/EN/LB workforce + 230k cross-border commuters: every venture launches EU-multilingual from day one — the localization capability *is* the studio's core asset |
| **Small-market discipline** | Luxembourg forces day-one thinking about the second market (the Rocket lesson institutionalized): every venture ships with its expansion rulebook (Ireland, Belgium, DACH) pre-scoped |

## 4. The machine — how Schmelz operates

### 4.1 Replica selection engine (the "assay")

Quarterly, the studio scores US models ≥$50M ARR or ≥$500M valuation against six
gates (full scoring in [`replica-pipeline.md`](replica-pipeline.md)):

1. **US proof** — revenue scale, retention, unit economics publicly evidenced.
2. **EU whitespace** — no EU native above €10M ARR in the category.
3. **Regulatory moat potential** — does EU law make the fork defensible?
4. **Luxembourg edge** — grants, credibility, talent, or SOPARFI-relevant.
5. **Capital intensity** — ≤€2M to contribution-margin proof.
6. **Original's EU intent** — is the US original visibly building EU-sovereign
   infrastructure? (If yes, score down or approach for partnership.)

Score ≥ 24/30 → 6-week validation sprint → go/kill.

### 4.2 Venture assembly line (per venture)

| Phase | Duration | Output | Kill gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assay | 6 weeks | Validation memo, 10 customer discovery interviews | Score < 24 or no design-partner interest |
| Foundry | 100 days | Incorporated SARL-S, MVP live, first design partner signed | No signed design partner |
| Ignition | 9 months | Founder-CEO recruited (EIR bench), €350k+ ARR path, Fit4Start filed | < 3 paying customers |
| Independence | by month 24 | Seed round led externally; studio steps to board seat | No external lead at market terms |

Studio equity at creation: **45–55%**, structured to leave founder-CEOs 25–35% post-seed
(the studio-equity failure mode — founders under-incentivized — is the #1 documented
reason studios fail; we price against it explicitly).

### 4.3 Shared platform (the actual moat of the studio)

- **Engineering chassis**: the Kloer scaffold generalized — EU-region infra,
  RLS multi-tenancy, hash-chained audit logging, EU-only AI gateway, rulebook-as-code
  framework. Every compliance-heavy replica starts 6 months ahead. (It exists today,
  in this repository, at [`/app`](../app).)
- **GTM playbooks**: regulated-B2B founder-led sales motion, 22/806/DORA procurement
  pack, design-partner contract kit — written once at Kloer, reused nine times.
- **Talent bench**: 4–6 EIRs (ex-operators from the fund industry and Big-4 advisory)
  cycling through ventures; shared recruiting, finance, legal.
- **Localization factory**: FR/DE/EN legal-linguistic pipeline for product, contracts
  and regulatory mapping — the single capability every replica needs and no US
  original has.

## 5. Portfolio construction (Vehicle I)

9 ventures over 4 years; expected outcomes calibrated to studio-industry base rates,
haircut for conservatism:

| Outcome | Industry base rate | Schmelz plan (of 9) |
|---|---|---|
| Killed pre-seed (cheap, <€300k each) | ~30% | 3 |
| Reach external seed | 72–84% of survivors | 6 |
| Reach Series A quality | ~72% of seeded | 4–5 |
| Fund-returner scale (€100M+ EV) | 1-2 per cohort | 1–2 |

At studio stakes of ~30–35% post-seed and ~22–25% post-A, one €150M-EV winner plus
two €30–50M outcomes values the holding at **€55–80M** against €8M deployed —
a 7–10× gross vehicle multiple over ~7 years, consistent with documented studio
TVPI (~5.8×) plus the replica de-risking premium.

## 7. Funding structure

- **Vehicle I: €8M** — €5M from 2–3 anchor family offices / LBAN syndicate
  (Luxembourg has one of Europe's densest family-office populations; a
  replica-studio with SOPARFI mechanics is a native product for them),
  €2M Digital Tech Fund / Luxembourg Future Fund co-investment track,
  €1M founders/GPs.
- Structure: SCSp (Luxembourg special limited partnership — the standard,
  fast, tax-transparent fund wrapper) over the SOPARFI holding; opco as SARL.
- Per-venture follow-ons beyond pre-seed are **externally led by design** —
  the studio never averages up its own marks (a second documented studio
  failure mode, priced against).

## 8. Risks (top 5 of the register)

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Studio-equity model deters top founder-CEOs | Founder economics floor (25–35% post-seed) + EIR salary during Foundry; publish the split policy |
| US originals enter EU faster than modeled | Original-intent gate in the assay; partnership option (become their EU build) preserved by non-hostile posture |
| Replica stigma with EU VCs at venture seed rounds | Lead with regulatory-fork framing and traction, not the analogy; Rocket-era stigma demonstrably didn't stop Zalando/HelloFresh follow-on |
| Two ventures fail loudly early, poisoning cohort narrative | Kill gates are cheap and quiet (Assay/Foundry, <€300k); publicize graduations, not experiments |
| Key-person concentration (2 GPs) | EIR bench from Y1; platform capabilities documented in-repo (this repository is the seed of that discipline) |

Kill criterion for the vehicle itself (pre-registered): if by end of Y2 no venture
has closed an externally-led seed at ≥€8M post, Vehicle I stops launching and
manages the existing portfolio only.
